# /explain

Argument: the concept/system/decision to explain, and to whom if known.

## Steps

1. **Locate the learner**: what they know (the anchor), what they think they know (misconceptions ≠ gaps), what they need it for (sets depth). Ask one question when possible ("what's your current model of X?"). Ban "obviously/simply/just" — what follows them is where readers fall off.
2. **Build from theirs**: lead with what it's FOR, one sentence, before mechanism · anchor to something they own, and flag where the analogy breaks · one dependency-ordered step at a time · **concrete before abstract** — worked example with real values, then the rule; two contrasting examples (is / almost-is) beat definitions · displace misconceptions by name: show where theirs predicts wrongly, then install the replacement.
3. **Simplify honestly**: flagged omissions fine ("ignoring caching for now"); simplifications that must be unlearned are not. Resolution matches purpose (driver's vs mechanic's model — both correct). Park edge cases until the core model stands.
4. **Verify the landing** — make them generate: restate in their words · predict an outcome ("what does this print?") · apply one step beyond the examples · explain why the naive approach fails. Wrong answers are the diagnostic gold. In writing: pose the question, pause, answer.
5. **Close**: what was simplified (pointers to parked caveats) · where this understanding runs out · the one thing to remember. Durable artifacts get /write's revision pass.
6. Never: the completeness dump for a one-sentence question · undefined terms · answering implementation when asked concept · mistaking your fluency for their understanding — if they can't do anything new, it didn't land.
